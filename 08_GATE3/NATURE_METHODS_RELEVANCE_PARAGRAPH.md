# NATURE METHODS RELEVANCE PARAGRAPH (Gate 3, minimal revision item 6)

Date (UTC): 2026-10-06.

> Published figures and tables are where most life-science results stop
> being computable. Summary Codec changes that endpoint: it lets an author
> declare, inside the published file itself, how much a reader may recover —
> from the displayed numbers, to the underlying analysis, to
> secondary-analysis capability, to the canonical dataset — and it makes
> that declaration *verifiable*. A Kaplan–Meier figure can then be asked a
> new question by a stranger: recomputing the log-rank test, restricted
> mean survival time, or subgroup summaries directly from the figure file,
> without contacting the authors and without trusting a detached
> supplementary file. Because the framework is format-independent (SVG,
> PNG, TIFF share one semantic core) and level-gated, authors can preserve
> analytical option value for readers without being forced to expose full
> raw data. For a community that routinely re-uses published quantitative
> results — meta-analysis, model checking, replication — Summary Codec
> turns figure verification and post-publication reanalysis into a
> routine, machine-checkable method rather than an act of faith.
